Two agent interfaces, two organizing ideas

The room vs the teammate.

Buzz organizes humans, agents, work, and evidence inside shared rooms. Grok Bot organizes work around durable named agents, each with a conversation and role, operating on one user-scoped cloud computer.

Buzz nucleus

Shared place

The room is the durable container. People, agents, patches, workflows, reactions, and decisions meet there.

Grok Bot nucleus

Persistent owner

The named Bot is the durable container. Its role, conversation, memory, routines, and results accumulate over time.

Common grammar

Conversation + action

Both mix human messages with tool activity, evidence, files, status, handoffs, and approvals in one transcript.

03 · Workflow shape

Where work becomes legible

Buzz · branch as room

1

A branch creates or anchors a shared room.

2

Patch, CI, review, reactions, and agent work arrive as events.

3

Humans and agents discuss the same evidence in context.

4

The merge decision remains searchable with the history.

Grok Bot · outcome owner

1

A named Bot receives an outcome, context, tools, and boundaries.

2

It works across apps on the shared persistent computer.

3

It hands off to other Bots or returns reviewable artifacts.

4

It pauses on consequential actions and asks for approval.

04 · Product direction

What this means for OpenSaddle

OpenSaddle should keep its outcome-and-governance center while borrowing the clearest interaction patterns.

Borrow from Buzz

Make context shared

  • Let a project thread hold discussion, runs, diffs, checks, and decisions together.
  • Treat agents as visible participants with identity and status.
  • Use one searchable activity grammar across artifacts.
Borrow from Grok Bot

Make ownership obvious

  • Give each agent a plain-language job, boundary, and current state.
  • Show tool activity and generated files inline.
  • Use direct, scoped approval cards with target and effect.
Keep distinct

Make governance the spine

  • Organize around user outcomes, not an expanding roster or channel tree.
  • Bind approvals to exact proposed changes and evidence.
  • Keep authority, execution, and verification states visibly separate.
